-3Aggregate as windows -Running Total And Average 


Select name , department , salary , 
   SUM(salary) over (Partition BY department) as dept_total_salary,
   AVG(salary) over (Partition by department) as dept_avg_salary
from employees;


--Normally, in SQL, if you use SUM() or AVG(), SQL squashes all rows into a single row to show the total.

Unlike a regular GROUP BY clause (which collapses rows into a single summary row), window functions allow you to keep every individual employee row while still displaying aggregate calculations (like total or average) alongside them.

1. SUM(salary) OVER (PARTITION BY department)

What it does: It calculates the total sum of salaries for each department and displays that total next to every employee in that department.

How it looks: If the IT department has 3 employees making 95,000, 90,000, and 90,000, the total sum is 275,000. Every employee in the IT department will have 275,000 shown in the dept_total_salary column.

Run Below Query..

AVG(salary) OVER (PARTITION BY department)

--How it works ? It calculates the average salary for each department and displays that average next to every employee in that department.

How it looks: It takes the total department salary and divides it by the number of employees in that department, repeating that average value for each employee in that group.


3--2. With GROUP BY (Department-Wise Summary)
Usually, when you want to see totals or averages per category (like per department), you combine normal aggregate functions with a GROUP BY clause.
